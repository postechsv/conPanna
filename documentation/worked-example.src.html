Certified unification modulo ACU · Worked example

Fruits in Baskets: One Certificate from Root to Leaves

Maude computes complete sets of unifiers modulo associativity, commutativity and identity (ACU). This page explains how such a set is certified complete: Maude writes a derivation in a small proof calculus, and Lean checks it. Part I introduces the calculus. Part II follows one certificate from its root to its leaves.

Part I

The calculus

1What is certified

A unification problem is an equation $s \stackrel{?}{=} t$ between terms with variables. Terms are built from free constructors (only equal when their arguments are equal) and from ACU operators: a union $\cup$ that is associative, commutative and has a unit $\varnothing$. Terms of an ACU sort are therefore finite multisets. A solution assigns values to the variables that make both sides equal.

A unifier $\sigma$ maps the variables to terms over fresh variables (its binders). Each value of the binders gives an instance of $\sigma$. A set $\Sigma$ of unifiers makes two claims:

Soundness. Every instance of every $\sigma \in \Sigma$ is a solution.
Completeness. Every solution is an instance of some $\sigma \in \Sigma$.

Soundness is a direct computation: substitute each unifier into both sides and compare normal forms modulo ACU. Completeness speaks about all solutions, of which there are infinitely many, so it needs a proof. Maude supplies that proof as a derivation: a finite tree of rule applications that Lean checks rule by rule. Maude is not trusted: a wrong derivation is rejected by the checker.

2States, judgments and rules

State
$\langle\alpha;\ E\rangle$. The list $E$ holds the equations still to solve. The substitution $\alpha$ records, for each variable of the original problem, the term it has become so far. A solution of the state is a valuation of its current variables that solves every equation of $E$. Through $\alpha$, it gives values to the original variables.
Judgment
$\langle\alpha;\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma$: for every solution of the state, the values $\alpha$ gives to the original variables form an instance of some unifier in $\Sigma$.
Claim
For a problem $E_0$, the root judgment is $\langle\mathrm{id};\ E_0\rangle \Vdash \Sigma$. It says exactly that $\Sigma$ is complete.
Normal form
Terms are compared modulo ACU by normalizing them: unions are flattened, $\varnothing$ is dropped, and the summands are sorted.

A derivation proves the root judgment with the seven rules below. They are read from the bottom up: to prove the judgment below the line, prove every judgment above it. A rule with nothing above the line closes its branch. The condition on the right is the side condition that the checker verifies.

Throughout, $f, g$ are free constructors, $x$ is a variable, $\epsilon$ is the empty list of equations, and $=_{\mathrm{ACU}}$ is equality modulo ACU. The equation a rule works on may sit anywhere in the list; it is written first only for readability.

Delete
$$\frac{\langle\alpha;\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}{\langle\alpha;\ s \stackrel{?}{=} t,\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}\qquad s =_{\mathrm{ACU}} t$$
Decomp
$$\frac{\langle\alpha;\ s_1 \stackrel{?}{=} t_1,\ \ldots,\ s_n \stackrel{?}{=} t_n,\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}{\langle\alpha;\ f(s_1,\ldots,s_n) \stackrel{?}{=} f(t_1,\ldots,t_n),\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}$$
Clash
$$\frac{}{\langle\alpha;\ f(\bar s) \stackrel{?}{=} g(\bar t),\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}\qquad f \neq g$$
Ground
$$\frac{}{\langle\alpha;\ s \stackrel{?}{=} t,\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}\qquad \begin{array}{l}\text{at some position reached through}\\ \text{free constructors only, } s \text{ and } t\\ \text{have different free constructors}\end{array}$$
Elim
$$\frac{\langle\alpha[x:=t];\ E[x:=t]\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}{\langle\alpha;\ x \stackrel{?}{=} t,\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}\qquad x \notin \mathrm{vars}(t)$$
ACU-Solve
$$\frac{\langle\theta_S\alpha;\ P_S,\ \theta_S E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma \quad \text{for every } S \in \mathrm{Sel}(B)}{\langle\alpha;\ s \stackrel{?}{=} t,\ E\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}\qquad \begin{array}{l}s, t \text{ of a multiset sort;}\\ \text{the basis } B \text{ passes the checks of §3}\end{array}$$
Cover
$$\frac{}{\langle\alpha;\ \epsilon\rangle \Vdash \Sigma}\qquad \sigma \in \Sigma,\ \ \alpha =_{\mathrm{ACU}} \sigma[w]$$

Notes on the rules:

  • Elim also applies to $t \stackrel{?}{=} x$. It replaces $x$ by $t$ in the remaining equations and in $\alpha$.
  • In Cover, the witness $w$ gives values to the binders of $\sigma$ in terms of the state's variables. Both $\sigma$ and $w$ are given in the certificate.
  • Ground is a shortcut for a run of Decomp steps ending in Clash.
  • In ACU-Solve, the basis $B$ is given in the certificate. $\mathrm{Sel}(B)$ is its set of legal selections, $\theta_S$ is the substitution of selection $S$, and $P_S$ are the equations it leaves pending. §3 defines all of these.

One small instance of each rule, with constants $a, b$, multiset variables $X, Y, W, Z, U$, and $g$ a binary free constructor:

3ACU-Solve

This is the only rule that reasons about multisets. It solves one equation $s \stackrel{?}{=} t$ whose sides are unions, by counting copies of elements.

Unknowns
The summands of the two sides, of two kinds. A bag variable is a variable of the multiset sort, and can hold any number of copies of an element. An alien is a summand that denotes a single element: a constructor term such as $f(Y)$ or $a$, or a variable of the element sort. A summand occurring identically on both sides may be cancelled; it then gets no unknown.
Row
Fix any element $e$. Write $v_j$ for the copies of $e$ in unknown $j$; for an alien, $v_j$ is $1$ if the alien equals $e$ and $0$ otherwise. The two sides contain $e$ equally often exactly when $\sum_j r_j v_j = 0$, where $r_j$ is how often unknown $j$ occurs on the left minus on the right. This single linear equation is the row.
Basis
The minimal nonzero solutions of the row, with alien entries at most $1$. A solution vector says how one element is shared among the unknowns. A basis vector with no alien is a way to share elements among bag variables; one with aliens says which aliens equal the element.
Selection
A set $S$ of basis vectors that contains every alien-free vector and covers every alien exactly once. Each selected alien-free vector $b$ gets a fresh bag variable $Z_b$: the multiset of elements shared the way $b$ says.

Each selection $S$ gives one premise. Its substitution $\theta_S$ maps every bag variable $x$ to the union, over the selected vectors $b$, of $b_x$ copies of the vector's representative: $Z_b$ for an alien-free vector, its first alien otherwise. Aliens that share a vector denote the same element. The pending equations $P_S$ equate each of them with that vector's representative.

The checker verifies four things.

  1. Row. The coefficients count each summand's occurrences, left minus right.
  2. Basis. Every basis vector is nonzero, solves the row, and has alien entries at most $1$.
  3. Completeness of the basis. Every nonzero solution lies componentwise above some basis vector. With coefficients in $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ this is a finite test. Each unit vector $e_j$ with $r_j = 0$, and each sum $e_i + e_j$ with $r_i = 1$, $r_j = -1$, must lie above a basis vector, unless $i, j$ are two aliens with different top constructors (they cannot be the same element). The test suffices because a nonzero solution $v$ has some $v_k \geq 1$. If $r_k = 0$ then $v \geq e_k$. If $r_k = 1$, the row forces some $v_j \geq 1$ with $r_j = -1$, so $v \geq e_k + e_j$; the case $r_k = -1$ is symmetric.
  4. Selections. Every legal selection is listed, and its fresh variables are new.
Why no solution is lost. Take a solution of the state. For each element $e$, its count vector solves the row. By check 3 it lies above a basis vector; subtract that vector and repeat, until the count vector is written as a sum of basis vectors. Every alien equals exactly one element, so it appears in exactly one of these decompositions, in exactly one vector. The alien vectors used therefore form, together with all alien-free vectors, a legal selection $S$. Let $Z_b$ collect the elements whose decomposition uses $b$, with multiplicity; an unused alien-free vector gets $Z_b = \varnothing$, which is why ACU's unit matters. With these values, $\theta_S$ gives every bag variable its value in the solution, and the aliens of each used vector are equal. So the solution is a solution of premise $S$, with the same values for the original variables.
Example
$X\cup\{a\} \stackrel{?}{=} Y$. Unknowns $(X, a, Y)$ with row $x + [e{=}a] - y = 0$, so $r = (1, 1, -1)$. The pairs to test are $(X, Y)$ and $(a, Y)$; the basis is exactly these two vectors, $b_1 = (1,0,1)$ and $b_2 = (0,1,1)$. The only legal selection is $\{b_1, b_2\}$. It gives $X := Z$ and $Y := Z\cup\{a\}$: every element of $Y$ is either one of $X$ or the $a$.

4Why a derivation proves completeness

Every rule has the same kind of justification. Each solution of a node's state is, after giving values to any fresh variables, a solution of one of its premises, and $\alpha$ gives the original variables the same values there. Clash and Ground close states that have no solution. Cover shows that the values $\alpha$ gives are an instance of an answer.

So a solution of the root travels down the tree, one rule at a time, until it reaches a leaf, and every leaf is covered by an answer. The tree does not have to say in advance which path a solution takes; each rule only guarantees that some premise receives it. The derivation is finite and given, so checking it is a terminating computation.

Part II

One certificate

5The problem

inductive Fruit  | a | b
abbrev    Fruits  := ACU Fruit       -- finite multisets of fruits
inductive Basket | f (x : Fruits)    -- f: a free constructor
abbrev    Baskets := ACU Basket      -- finite multisets of baskets

Variables: $X, Y, W : \mathsf{Fruits}$ and $R, S, T : \mathsf{Baskets}$. The problem asks for two multisets of baskets to be equal:

$$R\cup\{\underbrace{f(\{a\}\cup X)}_{A}\}\cup\{\underbrace{f(Y)}_{B}\} \;\stackrel{?}{=}\; S\cup T\cup\{\underbrace{f(\{a\}\cup W)}_{C}\}$$

The labels $A$, $B$, $C$ name the three baskets written in the problem; they are used throughout. A solution, followed down the tree in §10:

The baskets $B = f(Y)$ and $C = f(\{a\}\cup W)$ are equal exactly when their contents are equal, since $f$ is a free constructor. $Y$ does not occur in $\{a\}\cup W$, so Elim binds it.

8.2$C = A$: Decomp, ACU-Solve on fruits ($s_{11}$, $s_{12}$)

After Decomp, the pending equation is a multiset equation again, one level down, over fruits:

$$\{a\}\cup X \;\stackrel{?}{=}\; \{a\}\cup W$$

The alien $a$ occurs once on each side and cancels, so the unknowns are $X$ and $W$, with row $x = w$ (coefficients $(1, -1)$). The basis is the single vector $(1, 1)$: every fruit in $X$ is also in $W$. The completeness test has one pair, $(X, W)$, which is that vector. There is one selection, with fresh $Z_{15}$:

$$\frac{\dfrac{}{\langle\alpha_{13};\ \epsilon\rangle}\ \textsf{Cover}\ \sigma_6}{\langle\alpha_{11};\ \{a\}\cup X\stackrel{?}{=}\{a\}\cup W\rangle}\ \textsf{ACU-Solve}$$

where $\alpha_{11} = [R{\mapsto}Z_{13}\cup Z_{14},\ S{\mapsto}Z_{13}\cup\{f(Y)\},\ T{\mapsto}Z_{14}]$ and $\alpha_{13} = \alpha_{11}[X{\mapsto}Z_{15},\ W{\mapsto}Z_{15}]$.

Under $X, W := Z_{15}$, both $A$ and $C$ become $f(\{a\}\cup Z_{15})$.

9Cover

At $s_7$ no equation is left, and the state's substitution is

$$\alpha_7 = [X{\mapsto}X,\ Y{\mapsto}\{a\}\cup W,\ W{\mapsto}W,\ R{\mapsto}Z_9\cup Z_{10},\ S{\mapsto}Z_9\cup\{f(\{a\}\cup X)\},\ T{\mapsto}Z_{10}].$$

The answer $\sigma_4$ has binders $X, W, Z_1, Z_2$. The certificate gives the witness $w = [X{\mapsto}X,\ W{\mapsto}W,\ Z_1{\mapsto}Z_9,\ Z_2{\mapsto}Z_{10}]$, and $\sigma_4[w]$ is

$$[X{\mapsto}X,\ Y{\mapsto}\{a\}\cup W,\ W{\mapsto}W,\ R{\mapsto}Z_9\cup Z_{10},\ S{\mapsto}Z_9\cup\{f(\{a\}\cup X)\},\ T{\mapsto}Z_{10}] = \alpha_7.\ ✓$$

The checker compares the two in normal form, variable by variable. Every solution of $s_7$ is therefore an instance of $\sigma_4$: the binders take the values of $X, W, Z_9, Z_{10}$.

11The certificate as text

Maude writes the derivation in a text format, the Unification Derivation Format (UDF). An instrumented Maude prints one event per inference step of its own ACU unification algorithm. A converter rebuilds Maude's search tree from these events and writes it as UDF steps, and Maude's matching finds, for each leaf, the answer that covers it and the witness. Neither Maude nor the converter is trusted.

The certificate lists the problem, the answers, and one step per node. States are not written: the checker computes each premise state from its parent. Steps refer to equations by position (:eq 0) and to answers by name (a0 is $\sigma_0$).

12Checking it in Lean

In Lean, $\mathsf{ACU}\ S$ is the type of finite multisets over $S$, with $+$, $\varnothing$ and singletons $\{x\}$. A problem is a function from a tuple of variable values to a pair of sides, and each unifier is a function from its binders to a tuple. The two claims of §1 are the propositions complete and sound:

def problem : Problem Baskets (Fruits × Fruits × Fruits × Baskets × Baskets × Baskets) :=
  .bind fun (X, Y, W, R, S, T) =>
    (R + {f ({a} + X)} + {f Y}, S + T + {f ({a} + W)})

def answers : UnifierSet (Fruits × Fruits × Fruits × Baskets × Baskets × Baskets) :=
  [ .bind fun ((X, Y, W, Z₁, Z₂) : Fruits × Fruits × Fruits × Baskets × Baskets) =>
      (X, Y, W, {f ({a} + W)} + Z₁ + Z₂, {f ({a} + X)} + {f Y} + Z₁, Z₂),
    …   -- σ1 … σ7, as in §5
  ]

theorem complete : Unification.complete problem answers := by certify_comp
theorem sound    : Unification.sound problem answers    := by certify_sound

Both tactics work by proof by reflection. The rules of Part I are implemented once, in Lean itself, as a Boolean function check that takes a certificate and replays it. It computes every premise state, verifies every side condition, including the four checks of ACU-Solve, and returns true or false. A theorem proved once and for all says:

$$\texttt{check}\ c = \texttt{true} \;\Longrightarrow\; \texttt{complete}\ P\ \Sigma \qquad\text{for the problem } P \text{ and answers } \Sigma \text{ that } c \text{ describes.}$$

Its proof is the argument of §3 and §4, made formal. To prove complete for this problem, certify_comp proceeds as follows.

  1. It obtains the UDF text by running Maude and the converter.
  2. It turns the goal into data. The problem and the answers become syntax trees that mirror the Lean terms node for node, so the correspondence between the two holds by definition.
  3. It asks Lean's kernel to evaluate check on the certificate. If the result is true, the theorem above closes the goal.

certify_sound works the same way with a second function, which substitutes each answer into both sides and compares the normal forms; it needs no certificate.

What is trusted
Lean's kernel, the definitions of complete and sound, and the statement of the problem and the answers. Maude, the converter and the certificate are not: a wrong basis, a missing selection or a bad witness makes check return false, and the tactic fails. The two theorems above depend only on Lean's standard axioms.
The example is the Lean file producer/tests/udf/WorkedExample.lean. The derivation is produced by an instrumented Maude and checked by Lean.
